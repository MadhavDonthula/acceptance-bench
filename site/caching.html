Verdict — does caching help the acceptance loop beyond cost?

Yes — the help is latency, not just dollars. The loop re-sends a large, slowly-growing context every turn; a hit costs ≈0 ms of TTFT while a miss costs 9–25 ms/1k uncached, so on a ~30k-token prompt caching is the difference between a sub-second first token and a multi-second prefill (hit vs miss 0.30s vs 0.74s at 20k, widening to 0.91s vs 4.62s at 300k). It is durable (survives a 30-min pause), branch-friendly, and concurrency-friendly. The fragilities are an early edit near the prompt head (the cache quantizes to 256-aligned blocks, so an early change re-prefills far more than its position implies) and Coral's steep per-token miss penalty, which makes its high hit rate the thing buying the loop its speed. Keeping edits append-only preserves the benefit. All active conclusions are Coral-only by choice; the equivalent Baseten behavior is UNMEASURED.
